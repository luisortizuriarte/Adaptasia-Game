# Adaptasia Educator & Workshop Facilitator Guide

This guide is designed for high school teachers, university professors, and workshop leaders using **Adaptasia** to teach climate adaptation, urban planning, disaster risk reduction, and environmental science.

---

## Target Audience & Course Alignments

- **High School**: Environmental Science, AP Environmental Science (APES), Earth & Space Systems, Geography, Civics.
- **Undergraduate & Graduate**: Urban Planning, Civil & Environmental Engineering, Geography & GIS, Public Policy, Climate Resilience.
- **Professional Workshops**: Municipal staff, hazard mitigation planners, community resilience groups.

---

## Core Learning Objectives

After playing and debriefing with Adaptasia, learners should be able to:

1. **Differentiate Gray vs. Green Infrastructure**: Explain the trade-offs between hard-engineered concrete defenses (instant protection, high upkeep, brittle failure) and nature-based solutions (compounding co-benefits, lower maintenance, maturation lag).
2. **Analyze the Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect**: Describe how impervious asphalt and commercial buildings generate localized heat, and how tree canopy and wetlands provide evaporative cooling and microclimate moderation.
3. **Understand Non-Stationary Hazard Risk**: Recognize that historical weather patterns do not predict future climate extremes, requiring proactive planning for escalating downpours and heat waves.
4. **Identify the "Levee Effect" / Moral Hazard**: Articulate why constructing floodwalls often encourages risky development in flood-prone basins, exacerbating catastrophic losses when walls fail.
5. **Evaluate the Economics of Prevention**: Calculate and justify investments in pre-disaster mitigation using real-world data (such as the FEMA/NIBS 6:1 return on investment).

## Recommended 50-Minute Lesson Plan

| Segment | Time | Activity | Facilitator Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Hook & Introduction** | 5 min | Pose the framing question: *"If you are given \$25,000 to build a riverfront city, what is your top priority: rapid economic expansion or hazard protection?"* | Introduce the game URL or local server. Explain the 30-year planning horizon and win criteria. |
| **2. Guided Play: Years 1–10** | 10 min | Students build initial roads, homes, and shops on their own procedural map. | Prompt students to check the **FLOOD** and **HEAT** overlays before laying down dense housing. |
| **3. Hazard Escalation: Years 11–20** | 15 min | Floods and heat waves escalate in frequency and intensity. Disasters begin testing defenses. | Ask: *"Did anyone experience a levee breach or building abandonment? What happened to your tax revenues?"* |
| **4. Adaptive Strategy: Years 21–30** | 10 min | Students adjust their strategy: retrofitting green roofs, restoring wetlands, and managing upkeep costs. | Challenge students to reach 5,000 population while maintaining a resilience score $\ge 70$. |
| **5. Reflection & Debrief** | 10 min | Review the post-game **"Lessons Learned"** report and discuss key takeaways. | Compare gray vs. green spending ratios across the classroom. |

## Student Reflection & Discussion Prompts

### Question 1: Topography and Land Use
*Look at where your city suffered the most flood damage. Was it low elevation, near the river, or on the coast? Why did building on hills cost 1.5x more, and was that extra cost worth it?*

### Question 2: The "Levee Effect"
*Did you build levees or seawalls? What happened when water levels rose higher than 0.40? Did the levee protect your residents, or did it cause a worse disaster when overtopped?*

### Question 3: Nature-Based Solutions & Time Horizons
*Why is it dangerous to wait until Year 20 to start planting wetlands and shade trees? How does the game's multi-year maturation curve reflect real-world ecology?*

### Question 4: Public Health & Heat Equity
*How do cooling centers differ from tree canopy and green roofs? If cooling centers don't lower the temperature on the HEAT overlay, why are they still valuable?*

## Common Misconceptions to Address

1. **Misconception: "Higher concrete walls are always the safest solution."**
   - *Reality*: Walls create a false sense of absolute safety, encouraging dense development in low-lying hazard zones. When extreme storms exceed the wall's design threshold, the resulting breach creates catastrophic surge velocity.

2. **Misconception: "Green infrastructure works immediately upon installation."**
   - *Reality*: Seedlings, wetlands, and soil bioswales require establishment and root-maturation periods (modeled as 1 to 3 years in Adaptasia) before delivering full hydrological and cooling capacity.

3. **Misconception: "Cooling centers solve the urban heat island effect."**
   - *Reality*: Cooling centers are emergency life-safety shelters. They treat the symptom (heat illness) rather than the underlying cause (excessive thermal mass and absence of tree canopy).
